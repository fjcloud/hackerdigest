## Top Stories

### [Stealing Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLM APIs](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49257876)
Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google share an encryption key across their model families. An encrypted CoT block from a frontier model can be replayed into a weaker sibling, which the user then asks to recite the reasoning in plaintext. All three providers quietly fixed it after disclosure. The sharpest thread observation: pre-filling Kimi-K3 with Opus reasoning measurably shifts its style toward Opus, taken as evidence that Chinese labs already broke CoT encryption independently.

### [Mojo 1.0](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49261128)
Modular's Python-superset-for-GPUs hits 1.0, with first-class SIMD, MLIR-based compilation, and ownership semantics. The community reception is muted, mostly because the compiler stays closed until ModCon on August 18. The blog post is headlined by an AI-generated image that drew more attention than the language itself.

### [Go is an ideal language for AI-assisted software engineering](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49261133)
Google argues Go's small surface, mechanical error handling, fast incremental builds, and abundant training data make it ideal for coding agents. One counter-argument worth noting: Go's verbosity hurts AI agents more than human coders, since lost-in-the-middle effects punish dense prose harder than sparse prose.

### [Compression is prediction](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49263497)
ngrok's developer essay walks through why compressors and next-token predictors are mathematically equivalent, with the Shannon 1948 and MacKay 2003 lineages laid out. The sharpest thread critique points out this only holds when the training distribution matches future problems. In a non-stationary world the equivalence breaks and the argument collapses into vacuousness.
