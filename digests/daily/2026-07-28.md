## Top Stories

### [Anthropic argues against open-weight models under safety framing](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49076057)
Dario Amodei outlines three asks: tighter chip export controls, crackdowns on industrial-scale distillation, and mandatory safety testing for sufficiently capable models. The thread consensus was pointed: Anthropic built its models on copyrighted work, and now wants government protection from competitors doing the same. One commenter noted the recent OpenAI attack on Hugging Face makes the opposite case -- the open-source Chinese model was the only effective defense.

### [A $500 RL fine-tune of an open 9B model beat frontier models on a narrow task](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49078454)
FermiSense's GRPO-trained 9B model scored 87.3% on its catalog review benchmark versus 76.9% for the best frontier configuration. The thread's sharpest read: this trend scares closed labs. Frontier models stop being a moat when open fine-tunes match them on specific tasks at a fraction of cost. Commenters pushed back that the benchmark was tuned against directly, and patterns like this often lose to better prompting.

### [Watching Go's new garbage collector move through the heap](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49045474)
A visual walkthrough of how Go's GC handles same-size objects within contiguous spans. The practical takeaway is manually copying objects off occupied pages so the GC can release them. The best comment explains why fragmentation can't block large allocations: size-segregated heaps don't share space between small and large objects.
