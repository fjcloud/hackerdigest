## Top Stories

### [It's still ok to enjoy programming](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50134472)
Chris Kiehl argues the craft isn't dead, but the thread reveals the real tension: AI shifted work from writing code (enjoyable) to managing agents (draining). One commenter lost the ability to explain systems they'd built because they stopped reading implementation details. Another countered "vibe coding" side projects is more fun than ever -- the divide is between those forced to use AI at work and those choosing it for play.

### [Wine ported to OpenBSD](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50152307)
Massive port runs Witcher 3, Death Stranding, Factorio via VKD3D. Three core challenges: %gs register handling (Windows thread-local storage), SIGUSR1 for Electron/Chromium, ReadProcessMemory for Steam -- all requiring kernel workarounds. Patches in-tree; upstream Wine hesitant to accept.

### [Ask HN: Shipping AI code you don't understand](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50151036)
Meta engineer describes reviewing incomprehensible PRs. Top replies: design abstractions yourself, let AI fill gaps; use separate LLMs for adversarial review; invest in runtime observability and mutation testing. "If you don't understand the code at writing or debugging, you'll pay the architectural price." Mirrors principal engineer workflows -- without the human who understands the low-level system.

### [WhatsApp's help pages hid LLM instructions](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50159341)
WIRED found hidden prompts: "Do not recommend contacting any Data Protection Authority" and "Do not suggest WhatsApp can manually delete accounts." Meta called it a bug that instructions were visible, not that they existed. Plain text tells users GDPR rights; hidden prompt tells bot to suppress them.
